# SPEC M21: people, alien leaders, trading and a tidier screen

Designer 2026-11-21, after playing M20.

## Balance
- Births are 25% slower: a birth costs 25 food plus 6 per citizen past the first. The food store
  holds 75 (195 with the food depot).
- The map has 25% less area: 188 x 112 (was 216 x 128); continent boxes about 13% smaller,
  about 2,090 land hexes each. Each continent keeps its 15 towns.
- Freeing a town pays the liberator 25 gold for every citizen it held before the battle.

## People
- A newborn's stats are not known yet. The birth card shows the child and its chances: each
  stat pattern (+1 everywhere, +3 in two, +2 in three) and each domain's chance of being a
  strength. The town's focus domain is twice as likely to be picked.
- The stats are rolled when the child grows up. The growing-up card shows them and offers a
  job: work the best tile, join the council, or train as a soldier (opens Population).
- Hybrid children of the leader keep the stats they inherit at birth.

## Population as a side panel
- Population opens as a panel on the right, over the map, which stays in view. Each citizen
  shows a portrait, then name, traits and stats, what they do, and a job list: Auto,
  Unassigned, any council seat (or a named one), a tile, or training as a soldier.
- When a barracks is finished, the advisor says citizens can now train, and opens the panel.

## Alien town leaders
- Every alien-held town has a leader with a seeded temperament: bribable, greedy, aggressive or
  stoic. Scouts learn only a hint: "Fond of gold" (bribable or greedy), "Spoiling for a fight"
  (aggressive) or "Cold and patient" (stoic).
- Finding the town opens a scene with the leader (portrait, hint, what the town is rumored to
  hold) and, for those fond of gold, a bribe of 10 gold per point of the town's strength (at
  least 100).
  - Bribable: the leader defects to your council as a recruit and the town's defense halves.
  - Greedy: it keeps the gold and gives nothing. Either way it will not deal again.
- An aggressive leader turns on whoever finds it: 6 more heat (so more alien attacks) while its
  town stands.
- A town the aliens take gets a new leader; a freed town's leader is gone.
- The AI leaves leaders alone unless one is fond of gold and it holds twice the price.

## Rumors
- What a rumor means is spelled out: people (survivors to rescue), knowledge (science), arms
  (soldiers), wealth (gold), danger (a strong guard, maybe a hive heart: harder to take, but
  science and alien tech).

## Diplomacy
- First contact opens the talks with that civ at once, before the turn's other cards.
- Talks are with one civ: their leader in the hall, their items on the left, yours on the
  right, the deal in the middle. Items are gold and materials (timber, stone, iron), each sent
  once or every turn for 10, 20 or 30 turns. Materials come from the giver's richest towns and
  go to the receiver's capital.
- An alliance is not a trade item: it has its own button with what it means (shared sight,
  troops fed in each other's land, fighting together), and needs International Accords on both
  sides.
- The Diplomacy screen lists the civs, each with "Talk and trade".

## The screen
- The top bar keeps the readouts (turn, gold, science, beacon, threat, MVP, heat); every screen
  button moves into a Menu that opens as a vertical list.
- American English in all text players see. Internal names keep their spelling, so saves load.
